What to Buy in Japan:
My Top Picks

My favourite things to enjoy while you're there or bring home as gifts and souvenirs.

Item My tip Where to get it
Snacks and sweets
Nakamura Tokichi Cha Colate (matcha chocolate sandwich cookies) Our favourite sweet treat we brought home: deep matcha flavour in both the cookie and the chocolate, with a crisp, crunchy bite. Loved by locals but relatively undiscovered by tourists. They make an excellent gift. Next time we'll be sure to get a few boxes and try the hojicha version too! Where to get it: Nakamura Tokichi main shop, Uji (we bought ours there).
7-Eleven Choco-tto Gummy My favourite inexpensive treat in Japan! If KitKats aren't your thing, or you love gummy lollies, these are a great alternative. Fruity gummies coated in milk chocolate, for only about $1.50 AUD a bag. They come in several flavours, including Shine Muscat grape, pineapple and mandarin, with Shine Muscat being my favourite. Where to get it: Any 7-Eleven, so easy to find.
Itohkyuemon Uji Matcha and Hojicha KitKats Japan has so many KitKat flavours, but my advice is to pick one or two and save room in your suitcase for other souvenirs, as there are plenty of great things to bring home. My favourites are Uji matcha (different from the standard matcha one) and hojicha. They're very Japanese flavours, so they make a fitting gift. Where to get it: Easiest to find around Kyoto, and Don Quijote has the biggest range. You can also find a smaller selection at drugstores, train stations and airport gift shops.
Fujiya LOOK Shine Muscat chocolate Another tasty KitKat alternative. Shine Muscat grapes are hugely popular in Japan, and these smooth green muscat chocolates have a creamy Shine Muscat grape filling. Where to get it: Konbini and supermarkets.
Royce' Potatochip Chocolate Royce' is Hokkaido's famous chocolate maker. If you love a sweet and salty snack, you can't look past their potato chips coated in chocolate for a crunchy treat. Where to get it: Department store food halls, gift shops and airports.
Fujiyama Cookie If you're visiting Mt Fuji, these make a lovely snack or souvenir. Our favourite flavours were strawberry with sour cherry, and Earl Grey tea. For a gift, get a selection in the Mt Fuji-shaped tin, a cute souvenir in itself. Where to get it: Fujiyama Cookie shop, Kawaguchiko, near Mt Fuji. It's popular and some flavours sell out (we got some of the last sour cherry cookies!), so go early and be sure to check the opening hours.
Food to cook at home
Kyo Rayu Furikake A spicy chilli oil topping we both loved. Great on marinated eggs over rice. Light and easy to pack. We only bought one and it didn't last long, so next time we'll be sure to get a few. Where to get it: Loft (I found mine in Ginza).
CoCo Ichibanya curry packs A popular curry chain with branches all over Japan. If you're a fan, you can purchase packs in store, which make an easy gift for any Japanese curry lover, yourself included! Where to get it: CoCo Ichibanya restaurants and some supermarkets.
Beauty and skincare
Bioré UV Aqua Rich Aqua Protect Mist SPF50 PA++++ Japan has so many great sunscreens, and this is my favourite spray. The fine mist lets you top up your SPF throughout the day without ruining your makeup. For everyday cream sunscreens, I also love Bioré, Rohto Skin Aqua and Kosé Suncut. Where to get it: Drugstores like Matsumoto Kiyoshi, @cosme stores and Don Quijote.
Lululun face masks Japan's much-loved everyday sheet masks, designed to be used daily, so they come in great-value packs. There's a wide range for different skin needs and cute packaging, and the location-limited editions make great gifts. Where to get it: Drugstores, Don Quijote, @cosme stores and gift shops.
Majolica Majorca Lash Jelly Drop A clear lash serum that cares for your natural lashes and makes them look longer and fuller, even the tiny ones. I bought two and wish I'd got more, because I've run out! I'll definitely stock up next time. Where to get it: @cosme stores and drugstores.
Bioré Zero Cool body wipes Body wipes are really popular in Japan for freshening up on the go. These are large and thick, and soaked in cooling water with menthol, so they cool your skin instantly and wipe away sweat on hot days. We both loved them, but they were John's favourite beauty buy! Where to get it: Drugstores and convenience stores.
Homewares
Japanese ceramics Practical, beautiful and in so many designs, ceramics make a personal gift or souvenir. On Kappabashi Street, be sure to check out Dengama, with two floors of pottery from famous ceramic regions all over Japan. If you'd like to spend a little more, look out for local artisans' work as you travel. It's lovely to support them, and you can find something truly unique and special. Where to get it: Kappabashi Street, Tokyo, for the widest range and great prices.

For artisan pieces, Mashiko is a town famous for its pottery. It's on my list for next time!
Chopsticks A cute, practical and inexpensive gift. Some shops will even engrave your name for a lovely keepsake. Where to get it: Kappabashi Street has the widest range at the best prices. Don Quijote also has a large range at reasonable prices. For cute character chopsticks, try Don Quijote or character shops like Miffy Style.
Mini hand towels My favourite little souvenir, and now a collection! Many public toilets in Japan don't have paper towels or hand dryers, so lots of locals carry a small hand towel with them. I read about this before our trip and bought one, then realised how many cute designs there are, and now I collect them. They're light, inexpensive and easy to pack, so they make a lovely gift or keepsake. Where to get it: Gift shops at tourist spots often have their own designs (two of mine are from Farm Tomita and Miffy Oyatsudo in Kamakura). Character shops and department stores also have a big range.
Clothing and accessories
Sneakers My favourite sneaker store in Japan was atmos pink, a women's store made for street culture. It stocks a wide range of brands with the most unique designs, and the Shibuya store is an experience in itself! With its bold pink walls, glowing neon lights and a ceiling covered in circular mirrors, it feels like stepping into a futuristic dance-studio lounge. Where to get it: atmos pink Shibuya, Udagawacho, near Center-gai.
cas:pace phone cases Super creative, playful designs, some with moving parts, that make a fun souvenir or gift. I couldn't resist buying two! One looks just like a classic Japanese vending machine, right down to the tiny drinks and yen prices. The other is a claw machine with little bears floating in water, so you can tip one down like you've just won a prize! Where to get it: cas:pace tokyo, Harajuku (they also sell AirPod cases, phone straps, keychains and more).
Pre-loved luxury bags and clothing Japan is widely seen as one of the best places to buy pre-loved luxury, and for good reason, as items are so well cared for they often look brand new! For the best prices, head to smaller shops further out or to Osaka, which is usually cheaper than Tokyo. I got my bag at a great price at Atlantis Vintage in Koenji. Chain stores can be hit and miss, but you can find great bargains, especially on clothing. The more famous boutiques often have higher prices, as they tend to specialise in one brand or have a curated range of hard-to-find pieces. Either way, they're beautiful to browse and a great option if you're after something specific. Tip: lots of shops post their stock on Instagram, so you can look before you go. Where to get it:

For the best prices: Atlantis Vintage, Koenji (where I bought my bag!), Hedy, ALLU and Shinsaibashi-suji shopping street, Osaka.

For chain stores: Komehyo, Brand Off, Ragtag, Kindal and 2nd Street.

For the boutique experience: Vintage Qoo and Amore (great for Chanel).

On my list for next time: Brand Jungle (Osaka) and Hive Preloved (particularly to see their range of Fendi bags!).
Stickers and stationery
Stickers Loft was one of my favourite stores in all of Japan, and it's a great place for souvenirs and gifts, often with a whole floor just for stationery. Japan has the cutest (kawaii) stickers in so much variety, and you can find some really high-quality ones with thick paper and gold-foil details. Where to get it: Loft (Shibuya and Ginza are two of the biggest).
Postcards In the same way some people collect magnets, I collect postcards as a visual diary of the places I've travelled and little memories of each trip. They also make lovely gifts. Some of the places I've collected them from are Farm Tomita, the Sapporo TV Tower, Mt Asahidake, the Byodo-in Museum in Uji and around Mt Fuji. Where to get it: Gift shops at the places you visit, plus stationery shops, bookstores and Loft.
Stamps and ink pads A cute and practical gift for anyone crafty who loves scrapbooking or making cards, and you can use them again and again. I got some really cute stamps from the Studio Ghibli shop, and found them much cheaper in Japan than in Australia. For ink, I highly recommend VersaFine Clair in brown (Pinecone). It gives beautiful, crisp prints, and the pads even clip together and stack like Lego! Where to get it: Studio Ghibli shops (Donguri Republic) for stamps, and Loft for ink pads.
Cards, notepads and diaries Loft has a huge range of stationery, including lovely little cards and envelopes (some business-card size, perfect as gift tags), plus notepads, card papers and cute diaries. Where to get it: Loft.
